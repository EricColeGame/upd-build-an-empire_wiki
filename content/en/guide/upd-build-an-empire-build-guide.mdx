## The Core Gameplay Loop, Explained

Every session follows the same rhythm. Understanding it prevents the most common beginner mistake: spending your first profits on the wrong thing.

| Stage | What You Do | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Claim land | Start with a small piece of territory | Your land is the ceiling on everything else |
| 2. Build production | Place farms and mines | These generate the resources you sell |
| 3. Grow population | Add houses | Civilians staff your buildings and keep output moving |
| 4. Sell resources | Convert production into cash | Cash funds research, buildings, and troops |
| 5. Research | Unlock stronger buildings and bonuses | Multiplies everything you already own |
| 6. Build an army | Soldiers, Giants, Medics, siege weapons | Protects you and lets you take ground |
| 7. Fight and expand | Capture flags, control the map | More territory means more production |
| 8. Upgrade Empire Level | Push your overall rank higher | Unlocks the strongest buildings and bonuses |

Notice that combat sits at stage six, not stage one. New players who rush an army before their economy can support it end up with expensive units and no income to replace them.

## Early Game: Your First 30 Minutes

The opening stretch decides how quickly you snowball. Work through these steps in order rather than building whatever looks coolest.

| Step | Action | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Place your first farms | Get steady resource output running |
| 2 | Add at least one mine | Diversify beyond a single resource type |
| 3 | Build houses early | Population is what makes production actually tick |
| 4 | Sell surplus resources | Do not hoard — cash unlocks everything |
| 5 | Start your first research | Pick an economy upgrade before a military one |
| 6 | Add light defenses | A castle or two discourages easy raids |
| 7 | Only then build troops | Your first squad should defend, not conquer |

A useful rule of thumb from player experience: if you cannot afford to replace your entire army twice over, you are not ready to attack anyone.

## Economy and Build Order: Farms, Mines, Houses, Castles

Your plot is finite, so every building is a trade-off. The table below breaks down what each structure is actually for.

| Building | Primary Role | Build Priority | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Farm | Resource production | High, early | Cheap and fast; your first income source |
| Mine | Resource production | High, early | Usually slower and pricier, but valuable |
| House | Population growth | High, ongoing | Without population, production stalls |
| Castle | Defense and prestige | Medium | Protects your core and looks intimidating |
| Research buildings | Unlocks and bonuses | Medium-high | Feeds the upgrade tree |
| Military structures | Army capacity | Situational | Build only when you can sustain losses |

The balancing act is simple in theory: keep population slightly ahead of your production buildings, and keep production slightly ahead of your army. When one of those three falls behind, your growth curve flattens.

Community reports also suggest that spreading buildings out rather than clustering everything in one corner makes raids less punishing, since a single attacker cannot flatten your entire economy in one push.

## Research Priorities: What to Unlock First

Research is the multiplier layer. It does not add new income by itself, but it makes every farm, mine, and soldier you already own perform better.

| Research Focus | What It Improves | Recommended Order |
|---|---|---|
| Economy and sell value | Income per resource sold | First |
| Production speed | Output of farms and mines | Second |
| Population and housing | Civilian capacity | Third |
| Military strength | Troop stats and survivability | Fourth |
| Siege and siege weapons | Damage against structures and flags | Fifth |
| Empire bonuses | Passive advantages across the board | Whenever available |

An easy way to think about it: economy research pays for itself in cash, military research only pays for itself in territory. Early on, cash compounds faster.

## Army Composition: Soldiers, Giants, Medics, and Siege Weapons

Once your economy is stable, the army becomes your growth engine. Each unit type fills a specific slot, and a one-note army usually loses to a mixed one.

| Unit | Battlefield Role | Best Used For |
|---|---|---|
| Soldiers | Core frontline and cheap bodies | Holding lines, absorbing damage, raiding |
| Giants | Heavy hitters with staying power | Breaking fortified positions |
| Medics | Sustain and recovery | Keeping pushes alive during long fights |
| Siege weapons | Structure damage | Knocking down defenses and taking flags |

A practical composition tip: never send siege weapons in first. They are the most valuable and least durable part of your force. Let soldiers and Giants soak the initial contact, keep Medics behind the line, and bring siege weapons in only after defenses are softened.

Flag capture is the real objective in most fights. Killing units is a means to an end; controlling flags and holding map territory is what converts a won battle into a permanent income advantage.

## Empire Level, Bonuses, and Offline Progress

Three systems quietly decide how fast a veteran outpaces a beginner: Empire Level, bonus stacking, and offline production.

| System | How You Get It | What It Does |
|---|---|---|
| Empire Level | Ongoing progression and upgrades | Unlocks stronger buildings and bonuses |
| Premium bonus | Premium membership | Adds a sell value bonus (commonly listed as +5%) |
| Group bonus | Joining the game's group | Adds a sell value bonus (commonly listed as +10%) |
| Offline production | Simply logging off | Your empire keeps working while you are away |

The sell value bonuses are the most underrated part of the game. A percentage boost to every sale compounds across thousands of transactions, which is why joining the group is essentially free money for new players.

Offline progress is equally important. If you know you will be away for a while, queue up production and let the empire run rather than leaving your plot idle. Players who check in briefly, set production, and log out consistently out-earn players who sit in-game without a plan.

## Common Mistakes That Slow Down New Players

- **Building an army too early.** Troops cost money to replace, and early income is fragile.
- **Ignoring houses.** Population drives production; skipping housing caps your output.
- **Neglecting research.** Raw buildings without upgrades fall behind quickly.
- **Over-clustering buildings.** One bad raid can wipe an entire concentrated economy.
- **Forgetting the bonus systems.** Group membership and premium bonuses are easy multipliers to leave on the table.
- **Not using offline time.** Your empire is designed to keep working while you are gone.
- **Fighting for kills instead of flags.** Territory control is what actually pays.

Keep these in mind and the **UPD Build an Empire build guide** principles above will carry you from a single plot of dirt to a map-dominating empire far faster than trial and error.

## FAQ

**What is the fastest way to grow in [UPD] Build an Empire?**
Focus on farms, mines, and houses first, sell consistently, and invest your early cash into economy research. A strong income base makes everything else — army, castles, Empire Level — far cheaper to sustain later.

**Should I build an army right away?**
No. Build a small defensive force so you are not an easy target, then expand your military only after your economy can comfortably replace losses.

**Does my empire really keep working offline?**
Yes. The experience is designed so your empire continues operating while you are logged off, which is why setting production before you leave is one of the highest-value habits in the game.

**How do I increase my sell value?**
Stack the available bonuses. Premium membership and joining the game's group both add sell value percentage bonuses, and those percentages apply to every resource you sell over the life of your empire.
