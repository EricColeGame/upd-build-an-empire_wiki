## The Four Upgrade Pillars (and What Each One Actually Does)

Everything you invest in falls into one of a few buckets. Mixing them up is the single most common reason players stall out.

| Pillar | What It Covers | Main Benefit | When to Prioritize |
|---|---|---|---|
| Building upgrades | Farms, mines, houses, castles | Raw resource output and population capacity | First minutes of every session |
| Research | Unlocks stronger buildings and bonuses | Multipliers that apply empire-wide | As soon as the research menu opens |
| Empire Level | Overall progression tier | Access to stronger content and higher caps | Keep it climbing continuously |
| Army upgrades | Soldiers, Giants, Medics, siege weapons | Capturing flags and holding territory | Before any serious fighting |

The pattern is simple: buildings pay for research, research makes buildings better, Empire Level raises the ceiling, and the army protects all of it. Skip a link in that chain and the whole thing slows down.

### Buildings: Your Income Floor

Farms, mines, and houses are the foundation. Houses raise population, and a bigger population means more civilians actively working around your empire — which feeds directly back into production. Community reports consistently suggest that players who keep housing and production balanced level faster than players who dump everything into a single resource.

### Research: Where Real Multipliers Live

Research is the pillar most players underrate. A flat building upgrade gives you a fixed bump; a research unlock often applies a bonus across your whole empire or opens a stronger building tier entirely. That's why the best **UPD Build an Empire upgrades** advice is almost always "research first, build second."

### Empire Level: The Gatekeeper

Your Empire Level is the long-term scoreboard. It unlocks content, raises caps, and signals progress to other players. Treat it as a background objective — never stop feeding it, but don't obsess over it at the cost of the economy that funds it.

## The Best Upgrade Order: Early, Mid, and Late Game

Timing matters more than raw spending power. Here's a practical sequence that holds up across most patches.

| Phase | Priority 1 | Priority 2 | Priority 3 | What to Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early | Production buildings (farms, mines) | Cheap research nodes | Housing and population | Building an army too soon |
| Mid | Research multipliers | Empire Level progress | Storage and economy scaling | Over-investing in one resource |
| Late | Army and siege upgrades | Empire Level caps | Map control and flag pushes | Letting the economy stagnate |

A useful rule of thumb: if an upgrade doesn't either increase what you produce per minute or unlock something new, it can wait one cycle. Players who follow that filter tend to snowball, because every purchase funds the next one instead of sitting idle.

### A Step-by-Step Opening Routine

1. Place and upgrade basic production buildings so income never stops.
2. Open research and buy the cheapest nodes that boost production or sell value.
3. Add housing to raise population and put more civilians to work.
4. Re-check research — new nodes usually unlock as your Empire Level rises.
5. Only then start converting surplus resources into military units.

This loop takes a few minutes to learn and can be repeated indefinitely. It also scales well, since the same logic applies whether you're a new player or pushing late-game territory.

## Army Upgrades: Winning the Map, Not Just the Economy

An economy without an army is a donation to whoever attacks you. The experience offers a handful of distinct unit roles, and each one solves a different problem.

| Unit | Role | Best Use |
|---|---|---|
| Soldier | Basic frontline unit | Cheap numbers and early pressure |
| Giant | High-durability bruiser | Absorbing damage while others advance |
| Medic | Sustain and healing | Keeping pushes alive long enough to matter |
| Siege weapon | Structure damage | Breaking defenses before a flag capture |

Capturing flags is how you take control of the map, and flags rarely fall to a single unit type. A mixed composition — something to tank, something to heal, something to break structures — outperforms a stack of one unit almost every time. Upgrade your army only after your income can replace losses without stalling your economy.

### Balancing Upgrades and Army Spending

Community reports suggest the biggest trap in late play is over-militarizing. If you spend three sessions' worth of resources on units that die in one fight, you've traded permanent progress for temporary power. Keep a reserve, and only escalate military spending when you're actively contesting territory.
