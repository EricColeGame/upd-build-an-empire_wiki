## Economy Items: Farms, Mines, Houses, and Castles

Economy items are the engine room. Nothing else in the game — not research, not your army — runs without the resource flow they create. The community consensus is straightforward: get your first production buildings down fast, then upgrade them in waves rather than spreading thin.

| Item | What it does | Upgrade payoff | When to invest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Farm | Produces a steady stream of sellable resources | More output per collection cycle | Immediately after your first plot |
| Mine | Produces heavier resource output | Higher yield, better long-term value | Right after the first farm |
| House | Expands your civilian population | More workers active around your empire | As soon as production stalls |
| Castle | Raises prestige and defensive presence | Progress toward a higher Empire Level | Mid game, once income is stable |

Exact yields and upgrade costs shift with balance patches, so treat the table above as a priority order rather than a price list.

A few economy habits that consistently show up in player experience:

- **Build in pairs.** A farm without a matching mine leaves you short on one resource type while the other piles up.
- **Upgrade, then expand.** Two upgraded buildings usually beat four starter ones because your civilians can only staff so much at once.
- **Sell in batches.** Since sell value scales with research and bonuses, dumping resources constantly can mean leaving value on the table.
- **Watch your housing cap.** If production feels slow, the bottleneck is often population, not the building itself.

## Military Items: Soldiers, Giants, Medics, and Siege Weapons

Once your economy is producing, military items stop being optional. The game lets you fight other empires, capture flags, and take control of the map, and none of that happens with farms alone.

The unit roster described by the game breaks into four broad roles. Community reports on how each performs are consistent, though exact balance changes over time:

| Unit | Role on the field | Best used for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soldiers | Core infantry | Numbers, holding ground, early raids | Weak alone against heavy targets |
| Giants | Heavy frontline | Breaking through defenses | Expensive to replace in bulk |
| Medics | Support | Keeping pushes alive during long fights | Useless without a frontline |
| Siege weapons | Structure damage | Cracking castles and fortified positions | Slow, vulnerable without escorts |

A balanced composition beats a stack of one unit type almost every time. Giants absorb damage, soldiers fill gaps, medics sustain the push, and siege weapons finish what the rest started.

Flags are the payoff. Capturing them is how you expand your footprint on the map, and holding them usually requires a standing army rather than a one-time raid. ## Item Priority Plan: What to Buy First

Knowing which UPD Build an Empire items exist is one thing. Knowing the order to buy them is what actually speeds up your progress. This plan reflects general community strategy and can be adapted to your own play style.

| Stage | Focus items | Hold off on | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| First minutes | First farm, first mine | Military units | Income before army, always |
| Early game | Houses, second-tier economy buildings, cheap research | Castles and prestige items | Population gates your entire output |
| Mid game | Sell-value research, first castles | Overbuilding military | Percentage bonuses compound quietly |
| Late game | Giants, Medics, siege weapons, top research tiers | Idle spending on low-impact upgrades | Conquest demands a real army |

Additional habits that pay off across every stage:

- **Claim the free bonuses first.** Group membership and any available promotional perks cost nothing and stack with research.
- **Let your empire work offline.** The game supports offline progress, so a strong economy keeps earning while you are away. Build your economy up before a long break, not after.
- **Reinvest aggressively early.** The first hour is the cheapest time to buy production; costs scale as your empire grows.
- **Do not chase Empire Level at the expense of income.** Prestige looks good, but it is funded by a healthy resource flow.

Players who follow a rough order like this typically report reaching a stable, self-sustaining empire far faster than those who buy whatever looks shiny in the shop menu.

## FAQ

**What are the most important UPD Build an Empire items for a new player?**
Start with a farm and a mine, then add houses to grow your population. Those three cover income and workforce, which fund everything else — research, castles, and eventually your army.

**Do UPD Build an Empire items keep working while I am offline?**
Yes. The game is designed so your empire continues producing while you are away, which is why investing in economy items before a long break is one of the smartest moves you can make.

**Is the Premium or group bonus worth it for extra sell value?**
The group bonus is free and larger, so claim it immediately. Premium adds a smaller percentage on top and makes sense if you already play daily, since sell value affects every resource transaction.

**How do I decide which item to upgrade next?**
Ask what is actually blocking you. If resources pile up slowly, upgrade production. If production stalls, upgrade housing. If you are losing fights, upgrade research and military units instead.
